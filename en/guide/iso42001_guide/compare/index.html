ISO 5230, 18974, and 42001 Comparison

Compares the purpose, structure, and open source relevance of three standards: ISO/IEC 5230 (license compliance), ISO/IEC 18974 (security assurance), and ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management system).

1. Relationship Among the Three Standards

A company that develops AI systems using open source stands at the point where three standards intersect.

flowchart LR
    subgraph oss["Open source management"]
        A["ISO/IEC 5230\nLicense compliance\n(licenses, SBOM, artifacts)"]
        B["ISO/IEC 18974\nSecurity assurance\n(vulnerability detection, response, CVD)"]
    end

    subgraph ai["AI system management"]
        C["ISO/IEC 42001\nAI management system\n(governance, risk, life cycle)"]
    end

    oss -- "Open source compliance\nwithin AI systems" --> ai

ISO 5230 and 18974 are standards for managing open source itself, while ISO 42001 is a standard for managing AI systems. The two areas are independent, but they intersect when AI systems use open source.

ISO/IEC 42003, AI system implementation guidance (in development)

While ISO/IEC 42001 sets out requirements as principles, ISO/IEC 42003 is guidance (a newly approved work item) that explains specific ways to implement 42001. The OpenChain AI Work Group is working to have the AI SBOM reflected in the transparency and explainability clause of ISO 42003 (Appendix C.2.11, Transparency and Explainability). For the latest developments on mandatory AI SBOMs, see AI Work Group activities.

Priority for adopting the SC 42 family

Organizations preparing for ISO 42001 certification are recommended to review the SC 42 family standards in the following order: (1) 22989 (terminology) → (2) 23894 (risk) → (3) 42005 (impact assessment) → (4) 5338 (life cycle) → (5) 42003 (implementation guidance, once published). Using these five standards together with 42001 makes it possible to meet the level of specificity that certification auditors require without difficulty.

4. Comparison of Requirement Formats

The three standards express requirements in fundamentally different ways.

ISO/IEC 5230 and 18974 Approach: Numbered Verification Materials

Each clause specifies by number the verification materials (Verification Material) the company must provide.

§3.1.1 Policy
  Verification materials:
  - 3.1.1.1 A documented open source policy
  - 3.1.1.2 A documented procedure to communicate the policy

If the verification material exists, the requirement is ✅ met; if not, it is ❌ not met, so the judgment is clear.

ISO/IEC 42001 Approach: Management System “Shall” Requirements

Each clause presents requirements as principles in the form “the organization shall …”, and the organization decides, according to its context, which documents or records will satisfy them.

§5.2 AI policy
  "Top management shall establish an AI policy.
   The AI policy shall be appropriate to the purpose of the organization..."

For this reason, ISO 42001 is difficult to self-certify with a simple checklist as with ISO 5230/18974, and an internal gap analysis or an audit by an external certification body is needed.

7. Which Standard Should You Start With?

If you have no open source compliance framework

Start with ISO/IEC 5230. Build the foundation of license management, SBOM, policy, and training.

If you have an ISO 5230 framework and also develop AI

Review ISO/IEC 18974 + ISO/IEC 42001 in parallel.

  • ISO 18974: Strengthen management of vulnerabilities in open source used in AI systems
  • ISO 42001: Establish governance for AI systems as a whole (including the requirements that intersect with open source)

The two standards complement each other, so pursuing them at the same time reduces duplicated work.

If you are a company that develops and provides AI systems as services

Check the open source intersection requirements of ISO/IEC 42001 first. The Operation section of this guide describes the items you need to check right away in AI systems.

If you want to follow the trend toward mandatory AI SBOMs

Watch developments in ISO/IEC 42003. The OpenChain AI Work Group is working to have the AI SBOM compliance guide reflected in ISO 42003. The EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) is also being discussed in a direction that requires AI SBOMs as a means of transparency for AI systems.
